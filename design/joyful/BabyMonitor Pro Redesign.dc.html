BabyMonitor Pro — UI/UX redesign

v1 · LAN only

Built against HazemAfaneh/BabyMonitorPro@master — every state below traces to a real value in BroadcastState, VideoStatus or CameraEndpoint.Source. Copy is plain and flat: states as facts.

01 · CAMERA SCREEN STRUCTURE

Split it. Two destinations, not one scroll.

Confirm & Pair is the whole screen for the first two minutes: preview, state, address, QR. Camera settings is a second destination reached from a header action. The current single scroll makes the urgent, one-time job share vertical space with nine controls the parent touches once ever — and pushes the privacy note and the stop action below the fold on a 393pt phone.

02 · THE PRIVACY PROMISE

Make it a live state, not a paragraph.

One mint “On your WiFi · 192.168.1.42” line sits directly under the connection status on every screen, in the same slot, drawn from the same state. It reads as telemetry — this is where the bytes are going right now — which is a claim a parent can verify, unlike marketing prose. The paragraph shrinks to one sentence on the role picker only, where a first-time user is actually reading.

03 · AMBER ON CHARCOAL

Keep it — but stop spending it.

Amber is right for this product for a physical reason: long-wavelength light at low luminance is the least disruptive thing you can put in a dark bedroom, and it is the colour of every night light already in the room. The mistake is that primary currently paints the live dot, the pairing address, the sensitivity label and the alert banner — four unrelated meanings in one hue, so none of them signals. Amber is now reserved for “this is live and healthy”. Indigo takes interactive, mint takes privacy, error takes fault.

04 · NIGHT MODE

A second ColorScheme, not 45% alpha.

Modifier.alpha(0.45f) fades emitted light and legibility by the same factor, so the screen gets dimmer and harder to read at exactly the moment reading matters. Instead: drop the surfaces toward black (backgrounds lose ~60% of their luminance), pull chroma out of the accents, and keep text contrast above 7:1 against the new darker ground. Total light out of the panel falls further than the alpha trick achieved; the words get easier to read, not harder. Spec in 1g.

05 · TYPEFACE

No new dependency. Platform default stays.

Nothing in this design needs a specific face. One thing does need a tabular, unambiguous figure set — the pairing address, read aloud across a room — so it uses the platform monospace stack (FontFamily.Monospace), which every target already has. If you later want one face for brand reasons, IBM Plex Sans + IBM Plex Mono is the pairing I'd bundle; fallback FontFamily.Default.

06 · SEAMS I LEFT, PER SCOPE

Three, named and not designed.

The Find a camera list is a vertical stack of endpoint rows — a multi-camera grid later replaces the row with a tile, no restructure. The live view's bottom bar has one free slot to the left of Sound, which is where talk-back would sit. And the status chip's dot is a state enum, not a boolean, so a fourth recording state costs a token and no layout. None of the three is drawn.

44dp, top-right of the camera and live-view headers, next to the existing action so the thumb finds it without hunting. Filled moon = night is on. It writes one boolean to device-local storage — not to the shared broadcast state, so the two devices never fight over it.
WHAT NIGHT KEEPS, WHAT IT DROPS
Keeps — the six icons, every shape, every radius, all the layout. The screen a parent learned at 9pm is the same screen at 3am.
Keeps — the sound meter's motion. It is the only thing on screen whose movement carries information.
Drops — the four decorative tints (they collapse to one card surface), the marigold fills, press-scale, and the pairing animation. Settings at 3am is a rare visit; celebration is not wanted there.

WHY THE BANNER IS BUILT THIS WAY
Fixed slot, below the status chip. It occupies dead space above the picture, so the frame is never covered — the alert and the evidence are visible in one glance.
Berry outline, not a berry fill. A full red card at 3am is a light source. The 2px outline is legible at a glance and emits almost nothing.
One slide-in, then still. 10px down over 280 ms, and it stops. No pulse and no repeat: a pulsing alert in a dark room makes a parent's own heart rate the thing they notice.
“Got it” dismisses; it never auto-hides on its own after being read. Unacknowledged, it clears after 30 s so a stale alert can't be mistaken for a live one, and the event stays in the strip below.
Second event replaces the first and re-times it — stacking two banners would push the picture off screen, which is the exact failure the firm option was chosen to avoid.

One warm noun, and it now says where — which matters the moment a second camera exists. The metadata line carries what the other sensor saw, so a parent can judge whether to get up without leaving the picture.

THE RULE FOR ERRORS IN A JOYFUL APP
Joy never enters an error. No teddy on a failure, no “oops”, no softening of a fact a parent needs. The berry card is the plainest surface in the app.

2Joyful direction — a family palette and a small child-icon set
The one decision joy has to survive: this app is also used at 3am.
Joy is cheap to add in daylight and expensive at night — saturated colour on a bright ground is exactly what you cannot point at a sleeping baby's room. So joy is spent where the parent is awake and standing up: the role picker, pairing, finding a camera, settings. 2a takes the joyful scheme everywhere, including the live view. 2b keeps it by day and hands the night back to the dark scheme, carrying joy after dark in the icons and shapes instead of the light. What survives the switch to dark
The icons, the radii and the layout are identical — the teddy in the header, the footprint on the pairing card, the shield on the privacy line. That is what carries the family feeling after dark, and none of it emits light. What changes is only the scheme: Marigold → #C9924A, Blueberry → #7E88BE, Leaf → #5E9A85, Berry → #B06B62, ground → #07080C.
THE SWITCH
Not a manual toggle buried in settings. Joyful is the default; the dark scheme comes on automatically when the viewer device is in a dark room — Sensor.TYPE_LIGHT under ~10 lux, with the system dark-theme setting as the fallback where there is no sensor. The camera device follows the same rule, since it is sitting in the nursery with the lights off.
Why I would ship this one
Every joyful moment in 2a happens in daylight anyway — you set the camera up standing in the nursery, you pair a tablet on the sofa, you pick a role at bedtime. The only screen 2a makes brighter at night is the one screen a parent looks at in the dark. 2b keeps all the joy where it is felt and none where it hurts.

Proposed — nightColorScheme()
Nursery Pixel 8
PAIR ANOTHER DEVICE
192.168.1.42:8080
Broadcasting
Surfaces fall to near-black, borders replace fills to keep structure without emitting light, accents lose chroma. Text lands at 7.3:1 on the new ground — brighter relative to its surroundings than the day scheme, while the panel emits roughly a third of the light.
HOW IT ENGAGES
Keep the existing behaviour — settle 6s after the last touch, any touch restores the day scheme — but swap the scheme, not the opacity. LocalNightDim already exists and is already plumbed through BabyMonitorTheme; it now selects nightColorScheme() instead of being read by individual screens.

Two rules that come with it: the transition is a 400ms colour cross-fade with no movement, and it is exempt from LocalReducedMotion because a colour change is not motion. Nothing else in the night context animates at all.

Night applies to the camera device and the bedside viewer. It does not apply to the phone viewer picked up for a glance — that one is already dark and is only on screen for seconds.
